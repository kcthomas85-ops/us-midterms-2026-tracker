02 / A sweep with substantial margins

Eight major statewide races.
Every Democratic lead above 400,000 votes.

The Senate result was the largest of these margins, but the breakthrough reached the governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general, comptroller, land commissioner, agriculture commissioner and one Railroad Commission seat. The narrowest of these eight Democratic leads was still 437,831 votes.

03 / The history behind the headlines

Decades of political history
changed in a single election.

1988

The last Democratic Senate election win

Lloyd Bentsen’s reelection was the previous Democratic Senate victory in Texas. The gap to 2026 is 38 years. Bob Krueger served in 1993 by appointment, so “last Democrat elected” is the precise milestone.

Senate history ↗
1994

The last Democratic statewide wins

Democrats last won statewide elections in 1994. This result ends a 32-year losing streak across statewide contests—a much broader change than one Senate seat.

First

Texas elects a Latina governor

Gina Hinojosa defeated Greg Abbott to become governor-elect. She is the first Latina elected governor of Texas and the first Democrat to win that office since Ann Richards, who left office in 1995.

A Republican governing monopoly is broken

Democrats won the governorship and gained the Texas House. Republican control of the lower chamber had begun in 2003. Republicans retained the state Senate, so Texas moves to divided government.

Legislative result ↗

Four Supreme Court seats change hands

Democrats won all four Texas Supreme Court seats on the ballot, leaving a 5–4 Republican majority. They also won the three seats on the Fifteenth Court of Appeals. These change the decision-makers in consequential state litigation.

Winning the four available Supreme Court seats does not flip control of the nine-seat court.

05 / Why this is nationally significant

A result that changes
what both parties must consider.

A different U.S. Senate delegation

A Democratic senator elected from Texas changes congressional representation and the Senate balance of power. The symbolic weight is amplified by how long the state had gone without electing one.

A 40-electoral-vote question

Texas has 40 electoral votes for the 2028 presidential election. Our analysis: a statewide Democratic victory this broad gives both parties a reason to reassess resource allocation and the state’s competitiveness.

Real state powers change hands

The governor, House, attorney general and other elected offices exercise different powers. Those gains create routes to enforcement, negotiations and policy even with a Republican Senate.

The essential comparison

James Talarico’s early votes alone—4,397,474—exceeded Ken Paxton’s entire reported total of 4,249,373. He won Texas by nearly 1.2 million votes. Democrats also swept the seven major state-office races shown here, ending a 32-year statewide losing streak. This was a historic Texas breakthrough.

06 / Sources and calculations

The numbers behind the story.

Statewide vote margins use one consistent Texas SOS snapshot. The early-vote split is an independently displayed breakdown that adds back to the same Senate totals. Research reviewed November 8, 2026; figures remain unofficial.

How the comparisons were calculated

Vote lead: Democratic votes minus Republican votes. Point margin: that lead divided by all reported votes in the individual race, multiplied by 100. All-party denominators are used; figures are rounded only for display.

Early-vote claim: 4,397,474 early votes for Talarico minus all 4,249,373 Paxton votes equals 148,101. The “early votes only” chart mode removes only Talarico’s Election Day votes and leaves Paxton’s whole total intact.

Historical precision: first Democrat elected to the Senate since 1988, last Democratic statewide election wins in 1994, and first Democratic governor since Richards left in 1995. “Statewide sweep” here means the eight major contests in the margin table; it does not imply every county, legislative seat or local contest turned Democratic.

Count reconciliation: the SOS Senate total is 5,444,982 for Talarico. A different aggregator displayed a larger total; this page and the tracker’s Texas cards now use the SOS figures. Unofficial totals can still change before certification.
